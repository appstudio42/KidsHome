KidsHome turns a spare Android phone, tablet, or Amazon Fire into a home screen for a child. Parents choose which apps and websites appear, set a daily screen-time limit, and lock the device at bedtime. A PIN protects the parent area.

The app picks an enforcement mode for the device, or you can override it:

- Accessibility Home hijack for Amazon Fire tablets, which cannot replace the default launcher
- Default launcher on regular Android
- Device Owner lock-task, set up once with ADB on a device that has no accounts

The kid home shows large tiles, a remaining-time ring, and stars earned from simple tasks. Per-app limits and weekday schedules can narrow what is available. A weekly report stays on the device and can be exported as CSV.

Optional remote control serves a small page on the home Wi-Fi (port 8642). It is off by default, accepts only private LAN peers, and does not use a cloud account. From a phone browser, a parent can add time, lock, or unlock.

There are no ads, analytics, or Google Play Services. Website icons are loaded from the site you added. Screen time, the PIN, and usage history stay on the device.
